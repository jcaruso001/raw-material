Chapter One: Essential Facts

What is the SAT?

The Scholastic Assessment Test (SAT) is a college entrance examination developed and administered by the College Board. As of 2024, the SAT has undergone substantial changes to better align with modern educational practices and student needs.

Format and Timing:

  • Digital Testing: The SAT transitioned to a fully digital format in 2024. The test is administered through the Bluebook™ testing app, allowing students to take the exam on their own devices or provided devices at the test center.
  • Duration: The digital SAT is approximately 2 hours long, significantly shorter than the previous paper-based version.
  • Sections: The SAT now consists of two main sections:
    • Reading and Writing: Combined into a single section assessing comprehension and language skills.
    • Mathematics: Divided into two parts, one allowing calculator use and the other not.
  • Adaptive Testing: The SAT is now section-adaptive, meaning the difficulty of the second section adjusts based on performance in the first section.

Discontinuation of Subject Tests and Essay:

  • Subject Tests: The College Board discontinued all SAT Subject Tests in January 2021. These one-hour multiple-choice tests in specific subjects were phased out to simplify the testing process and due to decreased demand.
  • Essay: The optional SAT Essay was also eliminated in June 2021. This decision was made to reduce the burden on students and because many colleges no longer required the essay for admissions.

Scoring:

  • Score Reporting: Scores are now available in days, not weeks, providing quicker feedback to students.
  • Score Components: The SAT score includes separate scores for the Reading and Writing section, the Math section, and an overall total score.

Who Should Take the SAT?

The SAT is typically taken by high school juniors and seniors as part of the college admissions process. Alongside high school transcripts, extracurricular activities, recommendations, and essays, SAT scores are accepted by most U.S. colleges and universities.

Most students take the SAT for the first time in the spring of their junior year and may choose to retake it in the fall of their senior year to improve their scores before application deadlines.

Registration and Fees:

  • Registration: Students can register for the SAT online at www.collegeboard.org, where they can find test dates, deadlines, and testing locations.
  • Fees: The basic testing fee covers test administration and score reporting to up to four colleges of your choice. Fee waivers are available for eligible students through school counselors, including those who register online.

Chapter One: Essential Facts

What is the ACT?

As of 2025, the American College Testing (ACT) exam has been updated to a new enhanced format designed to provide a shorter, more focused assessment of college readiness while maintaining the same standards of fairness and reliability. The ACT now measures skills in three required areas—English, mathematics, and reading—with an optional science section and an optional writing test (previously known as “ACT Plus Writing”).

The ACT is designed and developed by ACT, Inc., following professional standards and codes established by the National Council on Measurement in Education, the American Psychological Association, the American Educational Research Association, and the Joint Committee on Testing Practices.

The enhanced ACT consists of 131 multiple-choice questions and takes about 2 hours and 5 minutes to complete. Students who opt to take the writing test will spend an additional 40 minutes. Because some colleges still require or recommend the writing test, you should check the admissions policies of the schools to which you plan to apply. Even if not required, completing the essay can show your willingness to “go the extra mile.”

Each required test area—English, mathematics, and reading—is scored on a scale of 1 to 36, with 36 being the highest possible score. These three scores are averaged and rounded to the nearest whole number to determine the composite score. If you take the optional science test, it will receive its own separate score and will not be included in the composite. Likewise, if you take the optional writing test, your essay will be evaluated by two trained readers and assigned a writing subscore, which is reported separately from your composite score. (For details about scoring, visit act.org/scores.)

Below is the format of the updated ACT exam:

Enhanced ACT Format (2025–2026):

  • English: 75 questions — 45 minutes
  • Mathematics: 46 questions — 60 minutes
  • Reading: 40 questions — 35 minutes
  • Optional Science: 40 questions — 35 minutes
  • Optional Writing (Essay): 1 task — 40 minutes

Who Should Take the ACT?

As a college entrance examination, the ACT is taken by high school juniors and seniors. Along with a record of your academic performance (the high school transcript), evidence of involvement in extracurricular activities, recommendations, essays, and other supportive documents — the ACT is accepted by the majority of the colleges and universities in the United States as a part of the application profile. The ACT is administered five times per year between October and June; however, some states also offer the exam in late September as well.

Since the exam measures mathematics skills through the third year of college preparatory study, we recommend that you take your first ACT no earlier than the spring of your junior year in high school unless you are highly advanced in your academic work. If you wish to try to improve your initial score, you should take the exam again in June or in the first semester of your senior year, prior to your college application deadlines.

How do I register for the ACT and what fees apply?

ACT test registration materials are available in your high school guidance or college counseling office. You will find test dates and registration deadlines posted in both of these locations as well.

You can also register online at www.actstudent.org/regist/elecreg, where you will find the testing dates for the current year. At this website, you will also find high school, college, and test center codes and information about eligibility for fee waivers and special circumstances related to ACT testing. Whether you register by mail or online, it is wise to read the registration material carefully, since other fees may apply under various conditions.

The basic fees for taking the ACT are outlined in the registration materials for the exam. These charges cover the administration of the test and your score report and allow you to send your scores to as many as four colleges.

Fee waiver information is also available through your guidance or college counselor, and fee waiver cards can only be procured from the counselor, even for online registration. Home- schooled students must provide the local high school proof of eligibility for fee waivers. To use the card, you must register for the ACT exams according to posted regular deadlines.
